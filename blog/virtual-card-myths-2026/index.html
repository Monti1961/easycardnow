Top Myths About Virtual Cards That Are Worth Clearing Up

Virtual cards are convenient, but there are still plenty of misconceptions about how they work. Some are harmless; others can lead to poor security decisions or unexpected costs.

Myth #1. A service can disappear together with my money

This concern is understandable. There are services with unclear ownership or terms, so users should always check who provides the service, where funds are held, what fees apply and what happens if an account is restricted.

A professional-looking website is not, by itself, a guarantee. Before depositing funds, read the provider's legal information, terms, KYC rules, limits and support procedures.

Myth #2. A virtual card is easy to lose

You cannot physically lose a virtual card because there is no plastic card to misplace. But you can lose access to the account or expose card details to fraudsters.

Protect your login, confirmation codes and card details. Never give anyone your password, one-time codes, private keys or seed phrase. If card data may have been compromised, contact the provider through an official support channel as soon as possible.

Myth #3. You cannot keep money available on a virtual card

It depends on the product. Some services use a prepaid balance, others use a payment account or convert funds before a transaction.

Check where the balance is held, whether funds can be withdrawn, whether there is a maintenance fee and what happens to any remaining balance when the card expires.

Myth #4. Virtual cards are only for small payments

There is no universal limit for all virtual cards. Limits are set by the specific provider or card programme and can include per-transaction, daily and monthly limits.

Some products may support larger payments such as flights, hotels or car rentals, while others have much lower limits. Always check the current limits of the specific card you use.

Myth #5. A virtual card works only online

Not necessarily. Some virtual cards can be added to compatible digital wallets such as Apple Pay or Google Pay, where supported. This can allow contactless payments in physical stores.

Availability depends on the product, country, wallet and merchant.

Myth #6. Virtual cards never require KYC

That is not a rule. Financial and crypto-related services may require identity verification and, depending on the situation, additional information about the customer or a transaction.

KYC can include an identity document, address information or source-of-funds information. Requirements depend on the provider and applicable law.

Myth #7. A virtual card is useless for travel

Virtual cards can be useful for booking flights, hotels, transport and other travel services. However, some hotels, car-rental companies and other merchants may require a physical card for deposits or pre-authorisation.

Myth #8. A crypto card and an ordinary virtual card are the same thing

Not necessarily. “Virtual card” describes the card format, while a crypto card usually refers to a product where digital assets are involved somewhere in funding or settlement.

A typical flow might look like: USDT → service balance → conversion → card balance → purchase. The exact model depends on the provider.

What should you actually check?

  • Who provides the card and which company is responsible for the service?
  • What are the issuance, top-up, conversion and transaction fees?
  • What are the daily, monthly and per-transaction limits?
  • How does KYC work?
  • What happens if an account or transaction is restricted?
  • How can you contact official support?

If you top up with USDT

Crypto top-ups add another risk: network and address errors. Before sending USDT, check the blockchain network, deposit address, minimum amount, fees and any stated validity period for the deposit details.

Crypto transfers may be irreversible, so verify the details before confirming a transaction.
